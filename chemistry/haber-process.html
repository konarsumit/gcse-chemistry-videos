Why 450°C and 200 atm? It's a compromise

The reaction between nitrogen and hydrogen to form ammonia is reversible and exothermic: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g). Le Chatelier's principle tells us the equilibrium position depends on both temperature and pressure, but the two conditions that would give the best yield don't give the fastest rate — so real industrial plants have to compromise.

Try switching the temperature and pressure controls above. Lower temperature and higher pressure both push the equilibrium towards more ammonia, so the yield goes up — but the reaction also slows down dramatically, since fewer molecules collide with enough energy to react. Higher temperature speeds the reaction up, but shifts the equilibrium the wrong way and lowers the yield. 450°C and 200 atm are chosen because together they give a reasonable yield at a rate that's fast enough, and a pressure that's cheap enough, to run profitably at industrial scale.
