Organic drawing board

Build hydrocarbons, cycloalkanes, aromatic rings, alcohols, carboxylic acids, aldehydes, ketones and esters yourself — every bond length and bond angle is drawn to the real chemistry, not just guessed. The molecule is a real 3D model: drag it to spin it round, or turn on auto-rotate, and save a picture once you're happy with the view.

How this works

Pick a molecule from the list, or type its molecular formula and let the board work out what it could be — some formulas (like CH₄) only have one possible structure, others (like C₃H₆) have several, and you choose which one to see.

Or build one yourself with the tools beside the board: start from a single carbon, then keep extending the chain. Only the actions that are chemically valid right now are switched on.

Quick-fire quiz
Bond lengths use real average values (in picometres). Cyclohexane is drawn as a genuine puckered 3D "chair" — rotate it to see the ring carbons alternate above and below the mean plane, which is what keeps its bond angles close to the tetrahedral 109.5° a flat hexagon (120°) can't give it. Smaller rings like cyclopropane are drawn flat, since any three points always lie in one plane anyway.
